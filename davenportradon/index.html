Iowa is the worst radon state in the United States, and it is not a close call. Every one of Iowa's 99 counties — Scott County included — is classified by the EPA as Radon Zone 1, the highest category, meaning the predicted average indoor screening level exceeds 4.0 pCi/L. The Iowa Radon Survey found 71.6% of Iowa homes above the EPA action level of 4.0 pCi/L, the largest share of any state, against a statewide average of roughly 7.6 pCi/L — nearly double the level at which the EPA says to act. The cause is geology, not construction: glacial till deposited over uranium-bearing bedrock releases radon that migrates up through soil and enters homes through foundation cracks, sump pits, and slab penetrations. A newer Davenport home is not automatically safer — a tighter building envelope exchanges less air, which can concentrate radon instead of diluting it.

What a radon mitigation system actually does

DRAFT — human editorial pass required before publish.

The standard fix for a Davenport home is active sub-slab depressurization. A suction point is cut through the basement slab, a sealed PVC pipe is run from beneath the foundation up and out of the house, and an inline fan runs continuously to pull soil gas out from under the slab and vent it above the roofline — before it can enter the living space. Sump pits are sealed and vented into the same system, and open foundation cracks are sealed so the fan pulls from the soil rather than from the room.

Scott County sits on glacial till laid over uranium-bearing bedrock along the Mississippi — the same deposit that makes eastern Iowa one of the most radon-prone corridors in the country. A correctly designed system usually brings levels well below 4.0 pCi/L, and often under 2.0. It runs on roughly the electricity of a low-wattage light bulb and needs almost no maintenance beyond watching the manometer gauge on the pipe. Any reputable installer performs a post-mitigation test after the system has run — a system that has not been re-tested has not been proven to work.

  • Active sub-slab depressurization — the EPA-recognized method for slab and basement homes
  • Sump pit sealing and crawlspace membrane systems where the foundation calls for it
  • Post-installation retest to confirm the level actually dropped
  • Systems vented above the roofline and installed to state credentialing standards

Davenport Radon Mitigation — FAQ

How much does radon mitigation cost in Davenport?

Most single-family homes in the Davenport and Quad Cities area fall in the $800–$2,500 range for a standard active sub-slab system. Price moves with foundation type, how many suction points are needed, and how the pipe has to be routed. Homes with multiple foundation sections, or crawlspaces needing a sealed membrane, sit at the higher end. Get the quote in writing with the post-install retest included.

What radon level requires mitigation?

The EPA action level is 4.0 pCi/L — at or above that, the EPA recommends fixing the home. The EPA also suggests considering mitigation between 2.0 and 3.9 pCi/L, because no level of radon is considered risk-free.

How long does the installation take?

Most Davenport installations are finished in a single day — typically three to five hours for a standard basement system. The fan can run immediately, and levels usually drop within 24 hours. The confirmation test is normally run at least 24 hours after the system starts.

Does a radon system make my house harder to sell?

The opposite. Radon testing is routine in Quad Cities real estate transactions, so a documented mitigation system removes a negotiation point rather than creating one.
