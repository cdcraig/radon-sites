Radon is invisible, odorless, and the second leading cause of lung cancer in the United States after smoking, according to the EPA. Berks County sits on the Reading Prong, the uranium-bearing formation that produced the 1984 Watras house at roughly 2,700 pCi/L and put radon on the national agenda. PA DEP reports about 40% of Pennsylvania homes test above the 4.0 pCi/L action level, with Reading Prong counties running higher. Because the Prong is fractured crystalline rock rather than uniform soil, results swing hard between neighbouring Reading properties — which is exactly why an individual test, not a regional assumption, is the only thing that tells you where your home stands.

Short-term, long-term, and real estate tests

DRAFT — human editorial pass required before publish.

A short-term test runs 2–7 days under closed-house conditions and is where most Reading homeowners start — fast, inexpensive, and usually enough to tell you whether to act. A long-term test runs 90 days or more and gives a truer annual average, since radon swings with season, snow cover, and how the house is being heated. Pennsylvania winters produce the highest readings: the house is closed, the stack effect draws hardest, and frozen ground pushes soil gas toward the foundation.

Real estate transaction testing is its own category. It is normally a short-term test performed by a third party under documented closed-house conditions, because the result becomes part of the negotiation. If a test is being run for a sale, it should be done by someone with no stake in selling the mitigation work afterward.

  • Short-term (2–7 day) tests for a fast answer
  • Long-term (90+ day) tests for a true annual average
  • Documented real estate transaction testing under closed-house conditions
  • Post-mitigation verification testing on existing systems

Reading Radon Testing — FAQ

How much does a radon test cost in Reading?

DIY short-term kits generally run $15–$40. A professional test with documented closed-house conditions — the kind used in a real estate transaction — typically runs $125–$250 in the Reading area. Pennsylvania certifies radon testers and mitigators through DEP; ask for the certification number.

When is the best time to test in PA?

Pennsylvania winters produce the highest readings: the house is closed, the stack effect draws hardest, and frozen ground pushes soil gas toward the foundation. You can test any time of year — but a low summer reading taken with the windows open is not a reliable all-clear.

Where in the house should the test go?

In the lowest level that is lived in or could reasonably be finished — for most Reading homes that is the basement. The detector sits 20 inches to 6 feet off the floor, away from exterior walls, drafts, vents, and humidity. Windows and exterior doors stay shut for 12 hours before and throughout a short-term test.

My neighbor tested low. Do I still need to test?

Yes. Radon levels differ dramatically between adjacent houses because they depend on that home's specific foundation, the soil and rock directly beneath it, and how the house moves air. Houses on the same block routinely test several pCi/L apart. An untested house is an unknown, not a safe one.
