Radon fans run continuously — every hour of every day — and they wear out, generally somewhere in the five-to-ten-year range. When a fan fails in Berks County, the house does not drift back to a mildly elevated level; it returns toward its untreated baseline. Berks County sits directly on the Reading Prong — a Precambrian uranium-bearing gneiss-and-granite belt that is the single most notorious radon formation in the United States. The failure is easy to miss because there is nothing to see or smell. The one indicator is the manometer — the small U-shaped gauge on the pipe. If the liquid in both legs has levelled out, the system is no longer pulling suction and the house is effectively unmitigated.

Common failures on Reading radon systems

The most common call is a dead or dying fan. A fan that has gone silent, or one that has become noticeably louder or started vibrating, is usually at the end of its bearings. Replacement is straightforward, but the replacement fan has to be matched to the original design — an undersized unit will not hold suction under the slab, and an oversized one wastes electricity and can pull conditioned air out of the house.

Other frequent problems: seal failures where the suction point meets the slab or around a sump lid, which let the fan pull room air instead of soil gas; cracked or disconnected pipe, often in an attic run or where the pipe exits; and systems that were undersized from the start and never actually got the house below 4.0 pCi/L. Any repair should end the same way an installation does — with a retest that proves the level came back down.

  • Radon fan replacement, sized to the original system design
  • Manometer diagnosis — confirming whether the system still pulls suction
  • Re-sealing suction points, sump lids, and foundation penetrations
  • Repairs and retesting on systems installed by other contractors

Reading Radon System Repair — FAQ

How do I know if my radon fan has failed?

Check the manometer — the small clear U-shaped gauge mounted on the pipe. If the liquid sits at the same height in both legs, there is no suction and the system is not working. Other signs: the fan has gone silent, or become noticeably louder or started vibrating. Note the gauge reading while the system is healthy so you have a baseline to compare against.

How much does a radon fan replacement cost?

In the Reading area, fan replacement typically runs $300–$700 installed, depending on the fan model and mounting location — an attic or roof-line mount takes longer than an accessible garage or basement position. Ask that the retest be included so you know the fix actually restored the system.

How long do radon fans last?

Most run five to ten years of continuous operation. Many manufacturers warranty the fan itself for around five years, though that generally does not cover the labour to swap it. Because they fail silently, checking the manometer a couple of times a year is the only reliable habit.

Can you work on a system another company installed?

Yes — this is a large share of repair work. The system gets inspected as designed and built: suction point placement, pipe routing and diameter, fan sizing, and seal integrity. If the original design was never adequate to get the home below 4.0 pCi/L, that gets identified rather than papered over with a new fan.
