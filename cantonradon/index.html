Stark County is classified by the EPA as Radon Zone 1 — the highest of the three zones, predicting an average indoor screening level above 4.0 pCi/L. Measured results bear that out: the Stark County average is roughly 6.42 pCi/L, more than half again the EPA's action level of 4.0 pCi/L. The Ohio Department of Health reports that about half of all Ohio homes test above that action level. The driver here is specific local geology: glacial till deposited over fractured shale and limestone formations containing naturally occurring uranium. Those fractures matter — they give soil gas a direct route toward foundations rather than dispersing it. Radon then enters a Canton home through slab cracks, sump pits, and utility penetrations, collecting in the lowest occupied level.

What a radon mitigation system actually does

DRAFT — human editorial pass required before publish.

The standard fix for a Canton home is active sub-slab depressurization. A suction point is cut through the basement slab, a sealed PVC pipe is run from beneath the foundation up and out of the house, and an inline fan runs continuously to pull soil gas out from under the slab and vent it above the roofline — before it can enter the living space. Sump pits are sealed and vented into the same system, and open foundation cracks are sealed so the fan pulls from the soil rather than from the room.

Stark County sits on glacial till over fractured shale and limestone — uranium-bearing rock whose fractures give radon a direct path toward foundations. A correctly designed system usually brings levels well below 4.0 pCi/L, and often under 2.0. It runs on roughly the electricity of a low-wattage light bulb and needs almost no maintenance beyond watching the manometer gauge on the pipe. Any reputable installer performs a post-mitigation test after the system has run — a system that has not been re-tested has not been proven to work.

  • Active sub-slab depressurization — the EPA-recognized method for slab and basement homes
  • Sump pit sealing and crawlspace membrane systems where the foundation calls for it
  • Post-installation retest to confirm the level actually dropped
  • Systems vented above the roofline and installed to state credentialing standards

Canton Radon Mitigation — FAQ

How much does radon mitigation cost in Canton?

Most single-family homes in the Canton and Stark County area fall in the $800–$2,500 range for a standard active sub-slab system. Price moves with foundation type, how many suction points are needed, and how the pipe has to be routed. Homes with multiple foundation sections, or crawlspaces needing a sealed membrane, sit at the higher end. Get the quote in writing with the post-install retest included.

What radon level requires mitigation?

The EPA action level is 4.0 pCi/L — at or above that, the EPA recommends fixing the home. The EPA also suggests considering mitigation between 2.0 and 3.9 pCi/L, because no level of radon is considered risk-free.

How long does the installation take?

Most Canton installations are finished in a single day — typically three to five hours for a standard basement system. The fan can run immediately, and levels usually drop within 24 hours. The confirmation test is normally run at least 24 hours after the system starts.

Does a radon system make my house harder to sell?

The opposite. Ohio licenses both radon testers and mitigation contractors — always confirm the license number before work starts, and keep the post-mitigation test result with your home records.
