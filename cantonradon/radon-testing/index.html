Radon is invisible, odorless, and the second leading cause of lung cancer in the United States after smoking, according to the EPA. In Stark County the baseline is already above the threshold for action — the county averages about 6.42 pCi/L against an EPA action level of 4.0, and the Ohio Department of Health estimates roughly half of Ohio homes exceed that level. Testing is the only way to know your own number. Because Stark County's radon comes up through fractured shale and limestone, readings vary sharply over short distances — one Canton home can sit well above the action level while the house next door tests low, depending on what the fractures beneath each foundation happen to do.

Short-term, long-term, and real estate tests

A short-term test runs 2–7 days under closed-house conditions and is where most Canton homeowners start — fast, inexpensive, and usually enough to tell you whether to act. A long-term test runs 90 days or more and gives a truer annual average, since radon swings with season, snow cover, and how the house is being heated. Ohio winters produce the highest and most conservative readings: the house is sealed, the stack effect is strongest, and frozen ground pushes soil gas toward the foundation.

Real estate transaction testing is its own category. It is normally a short-term test performed by a third party under documented closed-house conditions, because the result becomes part of the negotiation. If a test is being run for a sale, it should be done by someone with no stake in selling the mitigation work afterward.

  • Short-term (2–7 day) tests for a fast answer
  • Long-term (90+ day) tests for a true annual average
  • Documented real estate transaction testing under closed-house conditions
  • Post-mitigation verification testing on existing systems

Canton Radon Testing — FAQ

How much does a radon test cost in Canton?
DIY short-term kits generally run $15–$40. A professional test with documented closed-house conditions — the kind used in a real estate transaction — typically runs $125–$250 in the Canton area. Ohio licenses radon testers and mitigators, so ask to see the license number.
When is the best time to test in OH?
Ohio winters produce the highest and most conservative readings: the house is sealed, the stack effect is strongest, and frozen ground pushes soil gas toward the foundation. You can test any time of year — but a low summer reading taken with the windows open is not a reliable all-clear.
Where in the house should the test go?
In the lowest level that is lived in or could reasonably be finished — for most Canton homes that is the basement. The detector sits 20 inches to 6 feet off the floor, away from exterior walls, drafts, vents, and humidity. Windows and exterior doors stay shut for 12 hours before and throughout a short-term test.
My neighbor tested low. Do I still need to test?
Yes. Radon levels differ dramatically between adjacent houses because they depend on that home's specific foundation, the soil and rock directly beneath it, and how the house moves air. Houses on the same block routinely test several pCi/L apart. An untested house is an unknown, not a safe one.
